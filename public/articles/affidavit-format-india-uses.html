Affidavit Format in India: Types, Uses and How to Make One

By Ravomix Editorial · Published 2025-04-15 · Category: Legal

An affidavit is a foundational legal document in India, serving as an essential instrument for verifying facts and establishing truth in various administrative and judicial proceedings. Whether you are applying for a passport, correcting a name change, or filing a court case, understanding the correct affidavit format in India is crucial for ensuring your legal paperwork is processed without rejection.

What is an Affidavit?

In legal terms, an affidavit is a written statement of facts voluntarily made by an individual (known as the deponent) under an oath or affirmation administered by a person authorized by law. By signing an affidavit, the deponent declares that the contents of the document are true to the best of their knowledge and belief. In India, providing false information in an affidavit is a criminal offense under the Indian Penal Code (Sections 191, 193), which can lead to imprisonment or fines.

The primary purpose of an affidavit is to provide evidence in written form when it is not possible for a person to appear physically before an authority, or to record testimony in a standardized format for legal records. Since it is a sworn statement, it carries significant weight in government offices, banks, and courts across the country.

Essential Components of an Affidavit Format in India

While the specific content of an affidavit varies depending on its purpose, the basic structure remains largely consistent across India. A valid affidavit must contain the following structural elements:

  • Title: A clear heading indicating the purpose (e.g., "Affidavit for Address Proof" or "Affidavit for Name Change").
  • Identity of the Deponent: This includes the full name, age, father’s/husband’s name, and full residential address of the person making the declaration.
  • The Narrative/Facts: This is the body of the affidavit. It should be written in the first person (e.g., "I, the above-named deponent, do hereby solemnly affirm..."). Each fact should be stated in a separate, numbered paragraph for clarity.
  • Verification Clause: A concluding paragraph where the deponent confirms that the contents of the affidavit are true and that no part of it is false or concealed.
  • Signature and Date: The deponent must sign the document in the presence of an authorized official.
  • Attestation: The document must be notarized or attested by an Oath Commissioner, Notary Public, or a First Class Magistrate.

Common Types of Affidavits Used in India

The versatility of an affidavit means it is used in almost every sector of Indian administration. Below are the most common types encountered by the general public:

1. Affidavit for Name Change

This is frequently used when a person wishes to change their name due to marriage, numerology, or personal preference. It is the first step before publishing the name change in the Gazette of India. The affidavit must state the old name, the new name, and the reason for the change.

2. Proof of Address or Date of Birth

When a person lacks standard documents like a birth certificate or electricity bill in their own name, a stamp paper affidavit serves as a secondary proof for obtaining LPG connections, SIM cards, or local registrations.

3. Anti-Ragging Affidavit

Mandated by the University Grants Commission (UGC) in India, every student and parent must submit an affidavit during college admission, promising that the student will not engage in or promote ragging activities on campus.

4. Income Affidavit

Often required to claim scholarships, EWS (Economically Weaker Section) certificates, or reserved category benefits. The deponent declares their total annual income from all sources (salary, agriculture, business).

5. Affidavit for Loss of Documents

If you lose your original Marksheets, Share Certificates, or Sale Deeds, institutions require a sworn affidavit along with a Police FIR copy to issue duplicates.

The Role of Stamp Paper and Notarization

In India, most affidavits are legally valid only when executed on Non-Judicial Stamp Paper. The value of the stamp paper depends on the state where it is being executed and the nature of the transaction. For example:

  • General declarations for school/college often require a Rs. 10 or Rs. 20 stamp paper.
  • Property-related or high-stakes legal declarations may require a Rs. 100 or Rs. 500 stamp paper.

After the content is printed on the stamp paper, the deponent must take it to a Notary Public. The Notary verifies the identity of the signer (usually through an Aadhaar card or PAN card) and affixes their seal and signature. For court matters, the affidavit is often verified by an Oath Commissioner appointed by the High Court.

Pro-Tip: Since the introduction of the e-stamping system in India (managed by Stock Holding Corporation of India Ltd - SHCIL), you can now purchase stamp papers online in several states, making the process faster and more transparent.

Step-by-Step Guide to Creating an Affidavit

If you need to prepare an affidavit, following these steps will ensure your document is legally sound:

  1. Identify the Purpose: Clearly determine what fact you need to swear to. Is it for a passport, a joint bank account, or an heirship certificate?
  2. Draft the Content: Use clear, unambiguous language. Avoid jargon. Use the standard "I, [Name], S/o [Father's Name]..." opening.
  3. Purchase Stamp Paper: Buy the appropriate value of non-judicial stamp paper from a licensed vendor or an e-stamp center.
  4. Print the Draft: Have the drafted content printed on the stamp paper. Ensure there are no typos, as overwriting is generally not accepted in legal documents.
  5. Visit a Notary: Carry your original ID proof. Sign the document in front of the Notary. The Notary will enter your details in their register and affix the Notarial Stamp.

Legal Validity and Disqualifications

It is important to note that an affidavit is not "evidence" in the traditional sense under the Indian Evidence Act, 1872, unless the court specifically permits it or the law requires it. For instance, in criminal trials, oral evidence usually takes precedence. However, in civil suits and writ petitions, many proceedings are decided primarily on the basis of affidavits.

An affidavit may be rejected or deemed invalid if:

  • It is not properly signed or verified.
  • The deponent is a minor or of unsound mind (in such cases, a natural guardian must file on their behalf).
  • The stamp paper is of insufficient value or is an expired "Old" stamp paper from another state.
  • The facts are based on "hearsay" rather than personal knowledge (unless clearly stated as being based on information received).

Practical Example: Affidavit for Gap in Studies

Many Indian students face a situation where they take a year off to prepare for entrance exams like JEE, NEET, or UPSC. When they rejoin a formal course, colleges ask for a "Gap Certificate" or "Gap Affidavit." The format usually looks like this:

"I, [Student Name], age [X] years, do hereby solemnly affirm that I passed my Class 12 exams in the year 2022. I further declare that from July 2022 to June 2023, I was preparing for competitive examinations and did not join any formal educational institution during this period. I declare that during this gap year, I was not involved in any criminal activities."

This simple declaration, when put on a Rs. 20 stamp paper and notarized, fulfills the college's legal requirement for admission.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can an affidavit be used as a substitute for a Sale Deed?

No. An affidavit is a declaration of facts. To transfer ownership of immovable property, a registered Sale Deed is mandatory under the Registration Act, 1908. An affidavit cannot transfer title; it can only support claims regarding possession or the loss of original documents.

What is the validity period of an affidavit in India?

Technically, an affidavit does not "expire" unless it is for a time-bound purpose (like a temporary income certificate). However, most government authorities (like Passport Offices or Banks) prefer affidavits that are not older than six months to ensure the facts are current.

Who can attest an affidavit in India?

Affidavits can be attested by a Notary Public, an Oath Commissioner, or a Magistrate (Executive or Judicial). Some specific forms for government schemes may also allow attestation by a Gazetted Officer or a Tehsildar.

Can I make an affidavit on plain paper?

In most cases, no. Formal legal submissions require stamp paper to satisfy the Indian Stamp Act. However, during the COVID-19 pandemic, some courts allowed "self-attested declarations" on plain paper, but for general administrative work, stamp paper remains the standard requirement.

What happens if I sign a false affidavit?

Filing a false affidavit is considered "Perjury." Under the Indian Penal Code, this can lead to a prison sentence of up to 7 years. Additionally, any benefit obtained using that false affidavit (like a job or a property) can be revoked immediately.

Conclusion

An affidavit is more than just a piece of paper; it is a solemn pledge of truth that facilitates the smooth functioning of the Indian legal and administrative machinery. Whether you are using a standard affidavit format in India for personal reasons or professional requirements, ensuring the correct stamp duty and accurate verification is essential. By following the prescribed formats and legal guidelines, you can protect yourself from legal hurdles and ensure your applications are processed efficiently. Always consult with a legal professional if you are unsure about the specific wording required for complex judicial matters.
