Interview Call Letter & Job Application Letter Format (India)

By Ravomix Editorial · Updated 2025-04-15 · Category: Applications

Whether you are applying for a government job, a private-sector role or a campus placement, the right letter format creates the first impression. This guide covers the three letters every Indian job seeker needs — the job application letter sent to the employer, the interview call letter issued by HR, and the offer acceptance / joining letter you send back after selection.

1. Job Application Letter Format

A job application letter (also called a cover letter) is a formal letter attached with your resume. It tells the employer which post you are applying for and why you are the right candidate.

Standard structure

  • Sender's address — your full name, address, phone, email at the top.
  • Date in DD/MM/YYYY format.
  • Receiver's address — HR Manager / Hiring Officer, company name and address.
  • Subject line — e.g. "Application for the post of Junior Accountant — Ref: HR/2025/118".
  • Salutation — "Respected Sir/Madam".
  • Body — 3 short paragraphs: which post + source of vacancy, your qualifications and experience, request for an interview.
  • Closing — "Yours sincerely", signature, full name, enclosures (resume, certificates).

2. Interview Call Letter Format (Issued by Employer)

An interview call letter is sent by the company or HR department to inform a candidate that they have been shortlisted. It must clearly mention date, time, venue, mode (offline/online), documents to bring and a contact person.

Sample interview call letter

Ref: HR/INT/2025/0421
Date: 12 April 2025

To,
Mr. Rohit Sharma
Plot 14, Sector 9, Noida — 201301

Subject: Interview Call Letter for the post of Junior Accountant

Dear Mr. Sharma,

With reference to your application dated 28 March 2025, we are pleased to shortlist you for a personal interview for the post of Junior Accountant at our Delhi office.

Date: 22 April 2025   Time: 10:30 AM
Venue: 4th Floor, ABC Towers, Connaught Place, New Delhi
Documents: Original ID proof, all educational certificates, two passport photos, latest salary slip (if any).

Kindly confirm your attendance by reply email to hr@example.com.

Yours sincerely,
(Signature)
Priya Mehta, HR Manager

3. Offer Acceptance / Joining Letter Format

After clearing the interview, you must respond formally to the offer letter. A clean, polite acceptance protects you legally and confirms your joining date.

Key points to include

  1. Reference number and date of the offer letter.
  2. Clear statement: "I hereby accept the offer for the post of …".
  3. Confirmed joining date.
  4. Acknowledgement of CTC and terms.
  5. Polite thanks and signature.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Mixing casual WhatsApp tone with a formal letter.
  • Missing subject line or reference number.
  • Wrong recipient designation (writing "To Whom It May Concern" when HR's name is known).
  • Spelling the company name incorrectly — an instant rejection signal.
  • Forgetting to mention enclosures (resume, certificates, ID proof).

Interview preparation checklist (2025)

A well-written letter gets you to the interview — but preparation decides whether you walk out with an offer. Use this checklist for both government and private interviews:

  • Research the employer: read the company's About page, recent press releases and LinkedIn updates. For government roles, study the department's mandate and recent schemes.
  • Master your resume: be ready to defend every line. Quantify achievements ("reduced reporting time by 30%") instead of vague claims.
  • Prepare STAR answers: for behavioural questions, structure replies as Situation, Task, Action, Result.
  • Mock-interview yourself: record a 2-minute self-introduction on your phone and review it for filler words and pace.
  • Carry a document folder: two printed resumes, all original certificates with photocopies, ID proof, passport photos and a working pen.
  • Dress one notch above the role: for office jobs, formal shirt and trousers; for creative roles, smart-casual.
  • Plan logistics: reach the venue 20 minutes early; for virtual interviews, test camera, mic and internet 30 minutes prior.

Common interview questions to rehearse

  1. Tell me about yourself.
  2. Why do you want to join our company / department?
  3. What are your strengths and weaknesses?
  4. Describe a challenging situation and how you handled it.
  5. Where do you see yourself in five years?
  6. Why should we hire you over other candidates?
  7. Do you have any questions for us? (Always ask 1–2 thoughtful questions.)

What to do after the interview

The process does not end when you walk out of the room. Within 24 hours:

  • Send a short thank-you email to the interviewer, referencing one point from the conversation.
  • Note down questions you struggled with — use them for the next round.
  • If you do not hear back in the promised timeline, send one polite follow-up email after 7–10 days.
  • If selected, reply with a formal offer acceptance letter (template available in the Applications section).
  • If rejected, request feedback courteously — many HRs share it and it sharpens your next attempt.
